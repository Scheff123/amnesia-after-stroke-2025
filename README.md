🔬 Abstract  
Memory impairment following hemorrhagic and ischemic strokes presents a major challenge that significantly impacts patients' quality of life and their ability to adapt socially. Both types of strokes disrupt brain circulatory functions, causing neuronal death in memory-related areas. Ischemic strokes lead to tissue hypoxia, while hemorrhagic strokes cause brain compression and edema. This study investigates the mechanisms underlying memory loss after stroke and explores whether early interventions in intensive care can reduce cognitive deficits. The research, conducted in a hospital in a frontline-adjacent area of Ukraine, involved 15 patients aged 45 to 91 years with ischemic or hemorrhagic strokes. Cognitive function was assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE). Early rehabilitation techniques, such as monitoring cerebral perfusion, administering oxygen therapy, and applying neuroprotective treatments, led to improvements in memory preservation. The results underscore the importance of timely neuroprotective interventions to minimize cognitive impairments during the acute phase of stroke management.
